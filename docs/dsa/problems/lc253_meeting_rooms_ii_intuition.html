First Principles

How to see Meeting Rooms II as a peak-overlap problem

The answer is not about choosing meetings. It is about measuring the largest number of meetings alive at the same time.

LC 253Pattern 3Min-heap of active ends
Human Version

Every overlapping meeting adds pressure to room capacity

Core view

If three meetings are happening at the same time, you need three rooms right then, no matter what happens elsewhere in the schedule.

So the final answer is simply the maximum number of concurrent meetings.

Brute Force

The naive version checks overlaps over and over

You could, for each meeting, count how many other meetings overlap it, or simulate room assignment by scanning every current room every time a new meeting arrives. Both approaches keep redoing the same “which room frees first?” work.

Optimization

The earliest ending active meeting is the only one you need to inspect

Why the heap is enough When a new meeting starts, only the room whose meeting ends earliest has any chance of being reusable. All later-ending rooms are even less helpful.

That is why a min-heap of active end times solves the whole problem: it tells you the first room that becomes available.

Spot It

Look for “minimum resources for overlapping intervals”

Meeting rooms, hotel rooms, machines, exam halls, and servers often reduce to this same question: how many intervals overlap at peak load?

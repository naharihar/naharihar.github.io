First Principles

How to spot the hidden schedule frame in Task Scheduler

The main leap is to stop simulating every second and instead ask which task frequency is forcing the schedule to be as long as it is.

LC 621Pattern 3Frequency frame
Human Version

The most frequent task is the bottleneck

Why frequency matters

If task A appears five times and cooldown is two, those five copies force large gaps between themselves whether you like it or not.

Less frequent tasks mainly help fill the empty slots created by the most frequent tasks.

Brute Force

The naive idea is to simulate the CPU one time unit at a time

You could keep a set of currently available tasks, pick one, wait through cooldowns, and repeat. That is fine for intuition, but it makes the problem feel more complicated than it really is.

Optimization

Build the schedule around the task that appears most often

Optimization insight The task with maximum frequency creates the minimum possible frame length. Once that frame is known, the answer is either that frame or the total number of tasks if the frame gets fully filled.

That is why the formula only depends on maxFreq and how many tasks share that maximum frequency.

Spot It

Count first when identical tasks and cooldowns are involved

If the items are indistinguishable except for type and the constraints are based on waiting between equal types, frequencies often matter more than exact timeline simulation.

First Principles

How to arrive at the max-heap idea in Course Schedule III

The hidden question is not “which course should I take next?” It is “if I am over time, which accepted course hurts me the most to keep?”

LC 630Pattern 4Deadline order + repair step
Human Version

This is a packing problem with deadlines

What is fixed?

Each course has a duration and a latest finishing day.

What you control is which subset to keep and in what effective order to reason about them.

Brute Force

The naive plan tries subsets or reorderings

You could imagine trying every subset of courses and checking if it can be scheduled before the deadlines. That is correct in principle but far too expensive.

The first simplification is to sort by deadline, because if a course has an earlier deadline, delaying attention to it is rarely helpful.

Optimization

Whenever you are over budget, drop the longest accepted course

Why this repair works If your current chosen set cannot meet the latest deadline, removing the longest course buys back the most time. Any shorter removal would leave you with less remaining flexibility.

That turns the problem into a running process: add courses by deadline, and if the total time becomes too large, eject the largest duration from the chosen set.

Spot It

Look for “maximize count under cumulative budget”

When items each consume time, have deadlines, and the goal is to maximize how many survive, sorting by deadline plus a max-heap repair step is a very reusable pattern.

First Principles

How to know this problem needs two heaps

One heap answers “which room is free and smallest?” The other answers “if none are free, which busy room releases first?” Both questions matter independently.

LC 2402Pattern 3Free heap + busy heap
Human Version

This is room assignment with delays, not just room counting

Extra twist

In Meeting Rooms II, you only ask how many rooms are needed. Here, rooms are fixed, meetings may be delayed, and ties must choose the smallest room id.

So you need to simulate room state, not merely count overlap.

Brute Force

A naive simulation scans every room for every meeting

For each meeting, you could inspect all rooms to see which are free. If none are free, inspect all rooms again to find the one that becomes free earliest. That repeated scanning is the expensive part.

Optimization

Separate the “free now” question from the “frees earliest” question

Why two heaps appear naturally These are two different orderings. Free rooms should be chosen by room id, while busy rooms should be chosen by finishing time and then room id.

Once you separate those concerns, the two-heap design becomes almost inevitable.

Spot It

Use twin structures when the same objects need two orderings

If a system needs one ordering for available resources and a different ordering for occupied resources, it is often cleaner to maintain two heaps than to force one structure to do both jobs.

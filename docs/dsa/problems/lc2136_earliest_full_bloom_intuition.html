First Principles

How to reason about Earliest Full Bloom as “serial work plus parallel waiting”

The hard part is noticing that planting is sequential, but growing runs in parallel afterward. That changes what “best order” really means.

LC 2136Pattern VariantLongest tail first
Human Version

Each seed has two phases

Two-phase thinking

Planting consumes your time directly. Growing does not; it happens in the background after planting finishes for that seed.

So the total answer is the latest finishing “plant prefix + grow tail” over all seeds.

Brute Force

The direct solution is to try planting orders

You could permute the seeds, simulate planting days, then compute when each bloom happens. That is enough to reveal the structure but far too slow.

Optimization

Long growers should start waiting as early as possible

Exchange insight If one seed needs a much longer grow time than another, delaying it is costly because that long wait happens after your sequential planting work. Starting long growers earlier lets their waiting overlap more of the remaining planting.

That is why sorting by grow time descending produces the earliest final bloom day.

Spot It

Watch for one serial phase followed by a parallel tail

When tasks have a “do now” phase and then an independent waiting/processing tail, ordering by the longest tail first is often the right instinct.
